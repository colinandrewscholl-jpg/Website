About Colin

Colin Scholl is a Ph.D. Candidate in Experimental Psychology at the University of Texas at El Paso (UTEP), specializing in psycholinguistics, cognitive psychology, and discourse processing.

His primary research examines how readers construct, update, and maintain mental representations (situation models) when reading passages containing evolving character behaviors or unexpected event sequences. By comparing behavioral paradigms with millisecond-accurate eye-tracking metrics, Colin identifies the precise temporal dynamics of memory updating and cognitive conflict resolution.

In addition to discourse comprehension, Colin leads empirical projects investigating sentence-level language switching in Spanish-English bilinguals in the El Paso / Juárez border region, evaluating how executive control mechanisms adapt to high-frequency code-switching environments.

Research Program & Paradigms

Detailed breakdown of my active research program, including the empirical frameworks and experimental paradigms I employ.

Discourse & Situation Model Updating

When individuals read narrative texts, they construct mental representations of the events, characters, and settings described, or situation models. Colin's research evaluates how readers reconcile inconsistent trait information (e.g., a character previously described as "vegan" suddenly eating a "steak") in a dominant versus non-dominant language.

Primary Research Questions

  • How does initial trait encoding impact downstream character evaluations?
  • How does situation model updating unfold in bilingual texts?
  • What language-related factors modulate memory retrieval mechanisms of previously stated information in a text?

Methodological Approaches

  • Eye-tracking during free natural reading (First-pass vs. Total time).
  • Post-reading comprehension and character trait rating probes.

Bilingual Language Control & Switch Costs

In bilingual communities like the El Paso region, language switching is an everyday natural cognitive phenomenon. Colin's work analyzes the executive control demands associated with switching between Spanish and English in structured sentence contexts compared to unconstrained code-switching environments.

Key Hypotheses

  • Asymmetrical switch costs: Is switching to the dominant language more costly?
  • Syntactic alignment effects across English and Spanish clause boundaries.
  • Individual differences in habitual code-switching frequency.

Experimental Paradigms

  • Cued & Uncued sentence reading switch simulators.
  • Eye-movement tracking across intra-sentential switch boundaries.
  • Reaction time LMM modeling of participant language proficiency profiles.

Quantitative Methods & Open Science

Rigorous behavioral research requires advanced statistical techniques capable of handling nested participant and item variance. Colin utilizes Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMMs) and Bayesian estimation in R to analyze skewed reaction time distributions without data loss.

Eye-Tracking & Cognitive Load Measurement

Utilizing EyeLink 1000 systems to extract early and late eye-tracking metrics (First Fixation Duration, Gaze Duration, Regression Path Duration, and Re-reading Time) to isolate early perceptual processing from later cognitive integration.
